# Listening evidence for playback evaluation

Source: Floyd E. Toole, *Sound Reproduction*, third edition, sections 3.2,
3.4, 3.5.1.4–3.5.1.7, 4.6.2–4.6.5, 4.8, 12.3 and 17.3. Checked against
the supplied `books/toole3rd/toole_ocr` files 58–60, 66, 72–75, 101–116,
348–349 and 430–431. These are local OCR file indices, not printed pages.
This is a focused review, not a new cover-to-cover reading. OCR plot values
and equations have not been visually verified. The guidance is paraphrased;
the source files are not runtime dependencies.

## Define what the experiment can establish

- Detection asks whether conditions can be distinguished; preference asks which
  is preferred; attribute ratings ask how they differ. None alone establishes
  fidelity to an unknown recording reference. Lower prediction error is not a
  listening result, and a detectable difference need not be preferred.
- For a timbral comparison, control level and playback mode, use repeated,
  randomized, concealed conditions, and report listener and programme variation.
  Do not infer equivalence from a non-significant difference without suitable
  power and an explicit equivalence criterion.
- A single loudspeaker playing mono can expose timbral defects that are less
  differentiated in stereo/multichannel presentations (section 3.4). This is a
  useful diagnostic condition, not a replacement for evaluating spatial quality
  in the intended format. L+R playing identical material is two-source playback,
  not the single-source mono test; it adds acoustic interference.
- Include spectrally dense, sustained or reverberant material when examining
  resonances, alongside representative programme and suitably chosen transients.
  A clean solo voice or a single pleasant excerpt is not a comprehensive test.
  The most revealing stimulus depends on the defect and its frequency/Q.

## Control confounds without erasing the quantity of interest

- Keep absolute playback level repeatable as well as relative levels matched.
  State the matching signal, band, weighting, seat and method; different spectra
  can prevent one match from being exact for all programme material.
- Separate level-matched quality comparisons from measurements of useful output,
  dynamic headroom and limiting. Normalization must not hide output loss. When
  loudness itself is the independent variable, do not match away the effect.
- Test the same listener position or balance positions across conditions. Retain
  seat effects instead of pooling them away. Avoid discussion and response cues
  between listeners during a test.
- Training and repeated trials can improve consistency. Professional status,
  price expectations, age, and self-described experience do not establish
  discrimination performance. Record repeatability rather than declaring a
  listener reliable from a label.

## Listener population and source qualifications

Hearing thresholds alone do not describe frequency selectivity, temporal
processing, or binaural/spatial discrimination. For spatial release from masking,
speech-in-noise or immersive playback, specify the relevant listener population
and task; do not assume a normal audiogram guarantees identical performance.
Do not diagnose hidden hearing loss from preferences or listening-test results.

The book reviews particular experiments and makes some broad judgments about
age and hearing. Do not turn those into categorical participant exclusions or
invalidate an individual's preference. Define inclusion criteria before analysis
and report the population to which the conclusion applies. Use current clinical
and exposure guidance for health questions, not the book's historical exposure
examples as guarantees of safety.

For phase tests, distinguish absolute polarity/phase-only changes from relative
phase between sources. The latter can alter acoustic summation and magnitude
dramatically. Section 4.8's reports of subtle phase-only effects do not justify
ignoring crossover cancellation, and its quoted group-delay thresholds are not
universal limits for arbitrary frequency, stimulus, or listening conditions.
